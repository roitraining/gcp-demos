# 2.5 · Sampling

*Part 2 · Collect*

> [!NOTE]
> **Why you are here.** Tracing every turn is fine in a tutorial and expensive in
> production. This page keeps a fraction of traces with the standard OpenTelemetry
> sampler, and names the trap that fraction sets up for Part 3.

ADK installs no sampler of its own, so the SDK default `ParentBased(ALWAYS_ON)`
applies and the standard environment variables work unchanged. Keep half the
traces by setting the ratio sampler.

**👉 Do this.** Run 20 baseline turns under the ratio sampler and count the
traces that survived:

**Command:**

```bash
export OTEL_TRACES_SAMPLER=parentbased_traceidratio
export OTEL_TRACES_SAMPLER_ARG=0.5
```

Then fire the load and count roots. With the sampler off, all 20 turns keep their
spans; with it at 0.5, about half do:

**Expected output:**

```console
OTEL_TRACES_SAMPLER=default:                   kept 20/20 traces (140 spans)
OTEL_TRACES_SAMPLER=parentbased_traceidratio:  kept  9/20 traces ( 63 spans)
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **What it means.** The ratio sampler drops whole traces, not stray spans — 63
> is exactly 7 spans times the 9 turns it kept, so every surviving trace is
> complete. The decision is made once at the root and inherited by the tree,
> which is what keeps a sampled trace from having holes. Nine out of twenty is
> within the noise of a coin flip on a small run; over thousands of turns it
> converges on half.

Sampling is your main cost lever ([4.6](../part-4/index.md)): halve the sample,
halve the ingestion. But the `ParentBased` half of the default sampler is a trap
once Part 3 turns on propagation.

## Deep dives

### `ParentBased` and the trap ahead

`parentbased_traceidratio` is two rules: for a **root** span, keep it with the
given probability; for a span with a **parent**, do whatever the parent decided.
Locally every trace is its own root, so the ratio is all that matters. But once
[3.4](../part-3/index.md) makes ADK's spans children of an inbound request, the
parent's sampling decision wins — and if that parent arrived unsampled, ADK keeps
**nothing** for the request. A tutorial that turns propagation on without
thinking about the sampler loses almost every trace. 3.4's deep dive reproduces
that and fixes it with `OTEL_TRACES_SAMPLER=always_on`. For now, note that a
sampled-out turn still carries a trace id in its logs — an id that leads to a
trace Cloud Trace never stored.
